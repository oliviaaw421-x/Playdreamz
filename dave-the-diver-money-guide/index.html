Dave the Diver Money Guide: How to Make More Gold After the Early Game

Once the first few chapters are behind you, the early tricks stop moving the needle. A full menu and a well-poured tea still matter, but the big jumps in Dave the Diver income come from the systems the game unlocks later: researched recipes, the Fish Farm, Otto’s fields, trained staff and a second restaurant. This guide covers how restaurant money actually works and where the real gold is in the mid and late game.

Light spoilers: this guide names mid-game systems, VIP guests and side missions, but avoids story twists. Just starting out? Read our beginner’s guide first. It covers menus, tea and beer pours, hiring staff and the Cooksta ranks.

How Restaurant Income Actually Works

Nearly all of your gold comes from evening service, so it pays to understand what sets the price of a plate.

  • Enhancement level sets the price. Most regular dishes can be enhanced up to level 10. Each level permanently raises the price and taste, and costs extra copies of that dish’s ingredients. The cost grows with every level. Top dishes start at roughly 375–420 gold at level 1, and players report around 1,400–1,550 gold at level 10, so a maxed dish earns about three and a half times as much.
  • Each recipe makes a fixed number of plates. One batch of Takoyaki makes 6 plates, for example, while Green Humphead Parrotfish Curry makes 4. Expensive dishes with few plates can earn less in a night than a slightly cheaper dish with more.
  • Taste drives your Cooksta rank, not your price. “Best Taste” is simply your single highest-taste dish. One strong dish is enough to meet a rank’s taste requirement.
  • Catching fish alive pays. Fish caught alive with a net or tranquilizer (and lifted by the Salvage Drone if they’re big) come back at 3 stars, which gives you more and better meat than a harpooned fish.
  • New recipes come from research. You earn 1–5 Artisan’s Flame per shift depending on how well service went, then spend it in the Research tab. Some recipes can’t be researched at all and only come from story missions, VIP orders, Cooksta ranks or events.

The takeaway: pick a small number of high-value recipes you can keep stocked, enhance them all the way, and let cheaper filler dishes cover the rest of the menu.

The Most Profitable Dishes

Level 1 prices below come from fan-compiled game data (MeowDB). The level 10 prices are community reports, mostly from 2023, so treat them as estimates. Updates may have shifted the exact numbers.

  • Tropical Fish Sashimi is the standout mid-game pick. It’s a VIP reward, so you don’t need to research it, and its fish are easy to find in the reefs. Rice is the only bottleneck, which is why the rice farm below matters.
  • Dumbo Octopus Takoyaki tops the list but unlocks late, at a high Cooksta rank.
  • Skip anything built on expensive extras. Players report that truffle dishes cost so much to make that most of the profit disappears, and that egg and seaweed recipes are limited by how slowly those ingredients arrive.
  • Old “best dish” advice may be outdated. Vegetable Sushi and Skewered Cucumber were famous money makers at launch, but players reported they were nerfed in July 2023.

Build Your Own Ingredient Supply

Your best dishes are only as good as your stock. Farms let you keep top recipes on the menu without hunting the same fish every day.

Fish Farm

  • Unlock: the side mission “A Noisy Customer” starts on the first evening of Chapter 2. Serve Otto a Moray Eel Curry (2 Moray Eel and 1 Turmeric; morays come out at night) and he builds the farm behind the restaurant overnight.
  • How it fills: fish you catch sometimes drop eggs. The eggs go to the farm automatically and hatch the next day.
  • Breeding: two or more of the same species in a tank can breed, though some species breed much faster than others. Sell spares or send them to the restaurant to keep tanks from filling up.
  • Expanding: Otto sells new tank areas and extra capacity. Farmed fish can go to Bancho Sushi or to your branch.

Otto’s Fields

  • Rice paddy: opens after VIP guest Michael Bang’s mission. Otto only sells a little rice at a time, so growing your own is the reliable supply, and sushi sets like Tropical Fish Sashimi need it, so expand the paddy when Otto offers.
  • Vegetable patches: open after VIP guest Sammy’s visit. Buy seeds from Otto and water them. Players report that watering can upgrades cost 2,000 and 3,000 gold, and that the 4,000-gold sprinkler ends daily watering for good. That’s worth buying once you rely on onions or other vegetables for curry.
  • Chickens and seaweed: later in the story you can raise chickens for eggs and grow seaweed. Both are slower to produce than fish or rice.

Staff Who Pay for Themselves, and the Branch

Once the menu is strong, staff decide how much of it you can actually sell. These are the names fan guides recommend most often, based on their skills:

  • Bonus-plate skills are hidden profit. “Ingredient Prep” skills give a chance of an extra plate when a dish is cooked, which means more plates from the same stock.
  • Training gets expensive fast. Staff max out at level 20, but one reference guide lists about 2,700 gold for the step to level 10 alone (around 8,500 in total from level 1) and close to 100,000 gold for the final step to level 20. Each level also raises their daily wage. Train your core cooks and servers, and leave the bench at low levels.
  • Dispatch your spare staff. Staff waiting in reserve can be dispatched for a day to bring back seasonings like soy sauce, miso, olive oil and mayo. That saves you from buying them.

Opening the Branch

Winning the VIP mission “Bancho’s Ordeal? Pasta Contest!” unlocks a second restaurant. The hard part is the Humboldt Squid, which only appears on night dives. You can’t run the branch yourself. Instead, you assign staff and a manager, then pick the branch’s menu policy. The manager builds the menu from ingredients you send over from Bancho Sushi or the Fish Farm. A manager with higher total stats can use higher-grade ingredients, so give the job to a strong all-rounder.

VIP Guests and Party Nights

VIP Guests

VIP guests ask for a specific dish within a time limit. Serving it permanently adds the recipe to your menu, with no research needed. The ones that matter most for income:

  • Gourmet Vincent: wants a White Spotted Jellyfish caught alive, Sea Grapes and Salt. You’ll find both the jellyfish and the sea grapes in the Limestone Cave. Unlocks Seagrapes Jellyfish Sushi.
  • Michael Bang: unlocks Tropical Fish Sushi and opens the rice paddy. One of the most valuable missions for your long-term income.
  • Sammy: asks for Vegetable Sushi (Rice, Carrot and Eggplant) and opens the vegetable patches. If you miss him, he calls back a few days later.

Party Nights

Party invitations arrive by Mail a few days ahead. There are eight themes: Jellyfish, Tuna, Marlin, Shark, Cucumber, Curry, Shrimp and Lobster. After all eight, they come back in random order. Guests at a party glow, pay more for the themed dish, and some will only order dishes marked with the pink ribbon icon. Stock up on the themed ingredient before the night, and keep enough to cover every glowing customer.

Ranks, Running Costs and Cobra’s Shop

Is It Worth Ranking Past Gold?

Each Cooksta rank brings more customers (and, up to Gold, more menu slots), but also higher nightly running costs. According to fan-compiled game data, the restaurant’s operating cost jumps sharply after Gold:

Staff wages come on top of this. That’s why some players stay at Gold for a while. The trade-off is that higher ranks also bring more customers and unlock recipes like Dumbo Octopus Takoyaki, and players running a full Diamond setup report far bigger nights. Our advice: rank up once your menu has enough enhanced, well-stocked dishes to fill the extra seats. Don’t rush it.

Sell Valuables to Cobra

Cobra’s shop opens early in the story and buys the non-food valuables you find on dives. Players reported prices such as 500 gold for a Large Gold Bar, 200 for a Tiger Shark Tooth, and 100 each for Pearls and Moray Eel Jawbones. It won’t replace restaurant income, but it’s free money for things you’d otherwise just carry around.

Don’t Expect Decor to Pay

Restaurant decor is cosmetic. Players who tested it, including one who removed every interior item, saw no change in customer numbers or income. Decorate because you enjoy it, and only once your gear and kitchen are sorted.

Late-Game Gold

  • Catch sharks alive. A sleep-inducing sniper rifle, a tranquilizer or a steel net trap lets you bring sharks up with the Salvage Drone for 3-star meat, and more of it. This matters most before a Shark Party.
  • Plan night dives carefully. A night dive takes a big chunk of the evening, so the restaurant earns less that day. Go when you need night-only fish like Moray Eels or Humboldt Squid, not as a money run.
  • Use the deeper zones for top recipes. Several of the best dishes above, like Deep Sea Tempura and Ice Fish Curry, rely on fish from the deep and cold areas. Upgrade your suit and cargo box before you farm them.
  • Keep two restaurants stocked. With the branch running on Fish Farm supply, your main menu can focus on the dishes you enhanced the most.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most profitable dish in Dave the Diver?

Community reports put Dumbo Octopus Takoyaki at the top, at around 1,550 gold per plate at level 10, followed by dishes like Red Fish Roll and Deep Sea Tempura at around 1,460–1,480. These figures are mostly from 2023 and may have changed with updates.

Does decorating Bancho Sushi increase income?

No. Decor is cosmetic. Players who tested it, including removing every interior item, saw no change in customers or income.

Should I rank up past Gold on Cooksta?

Only when your menu is ready. Nightly operating costs jump from about 320 at Gold to 1,500 at Platinum and 2,500 at Diamond, plus wages. Higher ranks bring more customers and unlock recipes like Dumbo Octopus Takoyaki, so rank up once you have enough enhanced, well-stocked dishes to fill the extra seats.

How do I unlock the second restaurant?

Win the VIP mission “Bancho’s Ordeal? Pasta Contest!”. You’ll need a Humboldt Squid from a night dive. The branch is run by a staff manager, and stocked from Bancho Sushi or the Fish Farm.

How do I unlock the Fish Farm?

Complete the side mission “A Noisy Customer”. On the first evening of Chapter 2, serve Otto a Moray Eel Curry (2 Moray Eel and 1 Turmeric) and he builds the farm overnight.
